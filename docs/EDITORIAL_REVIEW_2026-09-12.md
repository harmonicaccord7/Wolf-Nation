## Shared decision tradeoffs

| Choice | Potential benefit | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Buy before the release | Exposure if the intended move starts immediately. | An adverse surprise, price gap or reversal can cause a loss. |
| Wait for the release | More information and less exposure to the first surprise. | A move may be missed or the later entry may be less attractive. |
| Stage exposure | Spread the timing of the decision. | Partial exposure can still lose value; multiple trades add costs. |
| Stay uninvested | Keep capital available for another opportunity. | Forego participation if the asset rises. |

Use your own horizon, risk budget and invalidation rule. The final decision remains yours.

## CPI scenarios

### CPI above expectations

An upside inflation surprise can increase the chance that rates stay restrictive, but the effect depends on the size, composition and market regime.

Possible mechanism: Real yields and the dollar may rise. Gold and crypto can weaken in that transmission path, but neither response is a rule; positioning, growth and liquidity can dominate.

- Wait for the release and initial rate/dollar reaction rather than guessing the number.
- Stage or reduce new risk if the user’s plan requires lower volatility.
- Compare headline, core and relevant revisions where available.

What weakens this interpretation: Rates and the dollar do not confirm the mechanism, or later data changes the policy path.

Sources: [BLS CPI release schedule](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm), [Federal Reserve research on macro surprises](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/how-markets-process-macro-news-the-importance-of-investor-attention.htm).

### CPI near expectations

A result near expectations may leave the market focused on details, revisions and the next policy communication.

Possible mechanism: The first price move can fade when the surprise is small or already priced in.

- Keep the existing plan and avoid forcing a trade.
- Watch the next confirmed catalyst.
- Use a smaller test only when the user has a defined risk limit.

What weakens this interpretation: A meaningful component or revision changes the policy interpretation.

Sources: [BLS CPI release schedule](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm), [Federal Reserve research on macro surprises](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/how-markets-process-macro-news-the-importance-of-investor-attention.htm).

### CPI below expectations

A downside inflation surprise can support a softer-rate narrative, but weaker growth can complicate the response.

Possible mechanism: Lower yields may support gold and risk assets; a growth shock or liquidity stress can reverse that relationship.

- Confirm the move across rates, dollar and the target asset.
- Prefer staged exposure and document the alternative explanation.
- Do not treat an educational scenario as a buy or sell instruction.

What weakens this interpretation: The move is reversed, or growth/liquidity evidence contradicts the soft-rate mechanism.

Sources: [BLS CPI release schedule](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm), [Federal Reserve research on macro surprises](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/how-markets-process-macro-news-the-importance-of-investor-attention.htm).

## FOMC scenarios

### More restrictive than expected

A higher path for rates or a more restrictive communication can tighten financial conditions.

Possible mechanism: Rates, the dollar and real yields may reprice together; risk assets can be more sensitive to the size and speed of the surprise than to the meeting label.

- Wait for the statement and press conference to be read together.
- Stage exposure only if the price response confirms the thesis.
- Keep a written invalidation level and position-size limit.

What weakens this interpretation: The statement, projections and market reaction point in different directions or liquidity is too thin.

Sources: [Federal Reserve FOMC calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm), [Federal Reserve monetary policy](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy.htm).

### Close to the expected path

An in-line decision can reduce one source of uncertainty, but it does not guarantee a directional move.

Possible mechanism: Markets may focus on guidance, growth language, balance-sheet policy or the next data release.

- Hold existing risk within the user’s plan.
- Compare the decision with the prior path before changing exposure.
- Avoid treating a calm first reaction as confirmation.

What weakens this interpretation: The press conference materially changes the expected path.

Sources: [Federal Reserve FOMC calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm), [Federal Reserve monetary policy](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy.htm).

### More accommodative than expected

A lower path or easier communication can loosen conditions, but growth concerns can offset the initial response.

Possible mechanism: The same announcement can support risk assets through lower rates or hurt them if it signals deteriorating growth.

- Wait for rates, dollar and risk assets to confirm the same mechanism.
- Use a staged decision instead of an all-at-once trade.
- Record what would make the thesis wrong.

What weakens this interpretation: The move is driven by a growth scare or reverses after the full communication.

Sources: [Federal Reserve FOMC calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm), [Federal Reserve monetary policy](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy.htm).

## PAYROLLS scenarios

### Employment growth stronger than expected

Compare the release with a timestamped expectation and examine payroll revisions, unemployment, participation and wage growth.

Possible mechanism: Stronger activity can support earnings while also delaying expected rate cuts. The growth and interest-rate effects can pull asset prices in opposite directions.

- Compare the growth interpretation with the rates and dollar response.
- Waiting preserves flexibility but can miss the initial move.
- Buying before the details are clear increases exposure to an adverse reversal.

What weakens this interpretation: The apparent strength depends on a temporary component or is offset by revisions.

Sources: [BLS Employment Situation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.toc.htm).

### Employment growth near expectations

An aggregate near expectations can hide a different story in payroll revisions, unemployment, participation and wage growth.

Possible mechanism: Component surprises and positioning may matter more than the headline; no directional response follows automatically.

- Compare the composition with the previous release.
- Keep an existing plan if the thesis has not changed.
- Allow for spread and transaction costs before making a small adjustment.

What weakens this interpretation: Components or revised history materially change the interpretation.

Sources: [BLS Employment Situation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.toc.htm).

### Employment growth weaker than expected

Check whether weakness is broad or concentrated, including payroll revisions, unemployment, participation and wage growth.

Possible mechanism: Easier-rate expectations may support some assets, while recession concerns can weigh on earnings and risk appetite. Weak growth is not automatically positive for Bitcoin or gold.

- Consider both an easing scenario and a growth-shock scenario.
- Staging limits initial exposure but does not eliminate loss risk.
- Remaining uninvested avoids that exposure while carrying an opportunity cost.

What weakens this interpretation: The weakness is revised away or the market prices a different growth/rate tradeoff.

Sources: [BLS Employment Situation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.toc.htm).

### Buying, waiting or staging exposure

Buying before a release creates exposure to an immediate move in either direction. Waiting gives more information but may mean missing a move or entering at a less attractive price. Staging divides the timing of exposure but adds costs and does not eliminate loss risk. Remaining uninvested preserves capital while carrying an opportunity cost. The final decision belongs to the reader.

Compare the release with a timestamped, sourced expectation and examine components and revisions. An increase from last month is different from an upside surprise. No consensus value, forecast probability or guaranteed response is asserted in this edition.
